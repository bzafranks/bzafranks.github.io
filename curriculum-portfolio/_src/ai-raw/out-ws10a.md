<div class="kicker">Teaching Slide 1</div>
<h2>What Is Point of View?</h2>
<div class="banner">Point of view is the author's <b>attitude, perspective, or position</b> on a topic. It is shaped by what the author noticed, who they are, and what they want readers to think.</div>
<div class="cards">
<div class="card"><div class="icon">👀</div><h3>Perspective</h3><p>What the author chooses to notice, describe, and focus on.</p></div>
<div class="card"><div class="icon">💬</div><h3>Word Choice</h3><p>Adjectives and verbs that reveal feelings, such as "delightful" or "wasteful."</p></div>
<div class="card"><div class="icon">🎯</div><h3>Purpose</h3><p>Why the author writes: to inform, persuade, celebrate, or question.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="foot"><span>Informational texts can still have a point of view.</span><span>1 / 8</span></div>

<div class="kicker">Teaching Slide 2</div>
<h2>Detective Clues: Read the Words</h2>
<div class="pair">
<div class="box pos"><div class="label" style="color:#1e7a44">Writer A</div>"The new bike lane is a <b>bold, smart step</b> toward a safer city."</div>
<div class="box neg"><div class="label" style="color:#a12a3a">Writer B</div>"The new bike lane is a <b>costly, reckless</b> mistake that slows traffic."</div>
</div>
<div class="clues"><h3>Look for these clues</h3>
<ul>
<li><b>Adjectives and adverbs</b> that praise or criticize (bold, reckless, wisely)</li>
<li><b>Which facts</b> the author includes, and which are left out</li>
<li><b>Who is quoted</b> and how their words are framed</li>
<li><b>The headline or title</b> and what it emphasizes first</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="foot"><span>Same topic, different word choices = different point of view.</span><span>2 / 8</span></div>
