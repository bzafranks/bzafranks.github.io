WS5 · Structured literacy intervention, Grade 3

Breaking Big Words

A five-week small-group intervention for third graders who read short words well but stall on long ones. Explicit, systematic and cumulative, with a defined response to every common error and clear rules for when to adjust or exit.

1. Who this is for

The Simple View of Reading (Gough & Tunmer, 1986) says comprehension depends on both word recognition and language comprehension. This intervention targets one specific word-recognition gap. Placing a student with a language comprehension difficulty in it would waste their time, so the entry screen checks both.

ScreenHowEnter this intervention ifRoute elsewhere if
A. One-syllable wordsStudent reads 20 words aloud (list below)18 or more correct (90%)Under 18: needs phonics work on single-syllable patterns first

Screen A words: ship, frog, best, cake, rain, park, coat, hide, much, jump, sled, bite, form, spin, chest, boat, light, sharp, mule, wish

Screen B words: rabbit, napkin, robot, cabin, music, tiger, hidden, sunset, planet, complete, invite, pumpkin, mitten, finish, lemon, bacon, insect, contest, unlock, rebuild

Screens are criterion checks written for this intervention, not normed tests. Schools using a universal screener should use it to identify candidates first, then use these to place.

4. Scripted lessons

Lesson 4 · Closed syllables, VC/CV division

Objective: I can split a word between two consonants and read each closed syllable. Standard: RF.3.3c.

Review · 4 min
Cards: short vowels a, e, i, o, u. "When a vowel is closed in by a consonant, it says its short sound." Students read: nap, kin, rab, bit, in, sect.
Teach · 6 min
"Watch me read a big word. napkin. First I find the vowels and mark them: a, i. Two vowels means two syllables. Between the vowels I see two consonants, p and k. I split between them: nap / kin. Each syllable is closed, so each vowel is short. nap… kin… napkin. Is napkin a real word? Yes."
Repeat with rabbit (double consonant: rab / bit). Then "We do" with insect and contest: students mark, teacher divides, all read.
Guided · 8 min
Students mark and divide on whiteboards: mitten, sunset, pumpkin, absent, tennis, picnic, goblin, plastic. Each reads one word aloud; group checks with thumbs.
Build · 4 min
Syllable cards: nap, kin, cat, nip, cab, in. "Make napkin. Change one card to make catnip. Now make cabin." (Preview of next week: cabin breaks the rule. Note it; don't teach it yet.)
Text · 6 min
Read "The Rabbit's Tunnel" (below): teacher model of the first sentence, choral reading, then partners alternate sentences.
Dictation · 2 min
"Spell sunset. Say the first syllable. Write it. Say the second. Write it." Then mitten.
Lesson 8 · Open syllables and the flex strategy

Objective: I can try a long vowel first when a single consonant is between vowels, and switch to a short vowel if the word doesn't sound right. Standard: RF.3.3c.

Review · 4 min
Closed syllables from Weeks 1–2; read rabbit, napkin, contest.
Teach · 6 min
"Here's a word with one consonant between the vowels: robot. When there is only one consonant, try splitting before it first: ro / bot. The first syllable ends in a vowel, so it's open, and the vowel says its name: rō. rō… bot… robot. Real word? Yes."
"Now cabin. Try it the same way: ca / bin. cā… bin… cābin? Is that a word? No. So I flex: move the line after the consonant: cab / in. cab… in… cabin. Yes!"
The rule for students: "Try long first. If it's not a real word, flex to short."
Guided · 8 min
Words: tiger, music, lemon, pilot, robin, open, seven, bacon. Students sort into "long worked" and "had to flex," then read each aloud.
Build · 4 min
Change one syllable: robot → robin; pilot → pilgrim (preview).
Text · 6 min
Short passage with open-syllable words, read chorally then with a partner.
Dictation · 2 min
tiger, lemon

Connected text for Lesson 4

The Rabbit's Tunnel

A rabbit dug a tunnel at the edge of a big garden. It was a snug spot, and the rabbit slept in it at sunset. In the hot sun, an insect hid in the tunnel too. A small moth crept in to rest on a dim, damp rock. The rabbit did not fuss. The tunnel was a hidden place for all of them, until the picnic in the garden ended and it was time to dig a bit more.

Original text. Target VC/CV words: rabbit, tunnel, sunset, insect, hidden, picnic, until. "Garden" (r-controlled) and "edge" are pre-taught as whole words until Week 4.

5. Corrective feedback

Errors are corrected right away, by pointing the student back to the strategy rather than giving the word.

ErrorExampleTeacher response
Guesses from the first lettersreads napkin as "nap""Count the vowels. How many syllables? Read each one." Student retries.
Wrong vowel sound in a closed syllable"nāp-kin""Is nap open or closed? Closed means the vowel says…?" (short)
Divides in the wrong place"na / pkin""Where are the two consonants between the vowels? Split between them."
Reads syllables correctly but cannot blend"rab… bit…" then stops"Say them faster: rab-bit, rabbit." Teacher models once, student repeats.
Doesn't flexreads cabin as "cā-bin" and moves on"Is that a word you know? What do we do when long doesn't work?"
CorrectanySpecific praise naming the strategy: "You found the two consonants and split between them."

6. Progress monitoring and decision rules

Every fifth lesson, each student reads a new 20-word multisyllabic list (three equivalent forms, all built from patterns taught to date) and a 100-word connected passage. Record accuracy on the list and accuracy on the target words in the passage.

If…Then…
List accuracy is 90% or higher on two checks in a row, and passage target-word accuracy is 90% or higherExit to whole-class instruction with a monthly check for six weeks.
Accuracy rises by at least 10 points between checksContinue as planned.
Accuracy rises by less than 10 points between two checksAdjust: check errors by pattern; reteach the weakest pattern; add five minutes of word reading; consider a smaller group.
No gain after an adjustment and a third checkRefer for a fuller diagnostic assessment of phonological awareness and decoding.
List accuracy is high but passage reading is slow or effortfulAdd repeated reading of connected text for fluency (RF.3.4); multisyllabic decoding may be accurate but not yet automatic.

No student data appears in this sample. The thresholds are starting points for a team to review against its own screener and local results.
