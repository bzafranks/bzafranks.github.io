Approach

How I design

A model earns its place when it changes a decision. Below, each model I rely on is paired with one decision it drove and the sample where you can see it.

My process in one paragraph

I start from the performance or reading outcome, not the content. I ask what learners must be able to do, what gets in their way, and what evidence would show they can do it. Then I design the assessment before the lessons, build practice that looks like the real task, and test the draft against a checklist and a real reader before it ships. Editorial review is part of design, not a step after it: accuracy, alignment, accessibility and consistency are checked at every stage.

Simple View of Reading (Gough & Tunmer, 1986)Reading comprehension is the product of word recognition and language comprehension. Weakness in either limits both.The Grade 3 intervention targets word recognition only, with entry criteria that screen out students whose difficulty is language comprehension, so they are routed elsewhere.WS5

Structured literacyExplicit, systematic, cumulative instruction with immediate corrective feedback.Intervention lessons follow a fixed routine with scripted teacher language and a defined response to each common error.WS5
Knowledge building through text setsComprehension depends heavily on background knowledge, which grows fastest when texts on one topic build on each other.The unit's texts are sequenced so each adds one idea the next one depends on: from one beaver dam, to engineers in many habitats, to effects that help and harm.WS1, WS2
Text complexity: qualitative, quantitative, reader and task (CCSS Appendix A)No single number decides where a text belongs.Every text has a three-part analysis. Readability formulas are reported as estimates, and qualitative judgment can move a text up or down a band.WS2
Backward design (Wiggins & McTighe, 2005)Decide the evidence of learning before planning the activities.The end-of-unit assessment blueprint was written before the lessons, and each lesson ends with a check that previews one assessed skill.WS6, Alignment
Gradual release of responsibility (Pearson & Gallagher, 1983)Move from teacher modeling to guided practice to independent work.The writing sequence models each sentence move before students try it with a partner and then alone.WS4
Retrieval practice (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006)Recalling information strengthens memory more than rereading it.Every lesson opens with a three-question retrieval warm-up on earlier texts, not a review of the last lesson's slides.WS1

ADDIEAnalyze, design, develop, implement, evaluate, with review at each stage.The mentoring course has a documented analysis, a design document approved before any build, and an evaluation plan written at the design stage rather than after launch.WS9
Action mapping (Moore, 2017)Start from the business or performance goal and the actions people must take, and include only content that supports those actions.The course goal is a mentor behavior (holding a specific, two-way feedback conversation), so practice takes more time than information.WS9
Merrill's First Principles (2002)Learning is promoted by real problems, activating prior knowledge, demonstration, application and integration.Each lesson follows that order: a real mentoring problem, the learner's own experience, a worked example, a branching scenario, then a plan for a real conversation.WS9 module
Adult learning principles (Knowles, Holton & Swanson, 2015)Adults bring experience, want relevance, and prefer to direct their learning.Learners plan a conversation with a teacher they actually support, and the live session starts from their cases.WS9
Cognitive load (Sweller, 1988) and multimedia principles (Mayer, 2009)Working memory is limited. Editorial quality

I review in passes, because one read cannot catch everything: accuracy and sources first, then alignment to standards and objectives, then pedagogy and clarity, then accessibility and bias, then style and conventions, and last, a proof of the final layout. Each issue is logged with a category and a severity so a team can see patterns, not just fixes. You can see this in WS7, and the team version in WS8.

How I use AI, with human review

AI tools are useful for first drafts, alternative phrasings, and finding things to check. They are not reliable for facts, standards wording, readability levels or citations, so I verify each of those against the source. In this section, AI output appears only where it is the object of review (WS7 and WS10), and it is shown exactly as generated, with the prompt.
