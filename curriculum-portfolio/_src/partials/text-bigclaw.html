Big Claw's Burrow

Original text by Barbara Zá Franks · 307 words

1Big Claw was the largest land crab on Pine Lane. Every evening, when the sun slid behind the casuarina trees, she climbed out of her burrow to look for fallen sea grapes.

2Her burrow was her pride. She had dug it herself, one scoop of sand at a time, down and down until the walls felt cool and damp. At the bottom, there was even a little water. On hot days, Big Claw rested there while the rest of Grand Bahama baked in the sun.

3"This burrow is mine," she told everyone who passed. "I dug it. Nobody else comes in."

4One afternoon, the sky turned the color of an old bruise. The wind pushed the palm trees sideways. Rain came down so hard that it bounced.

5Big Claw hurried to her burrow. At the entrance she found a curly-tailed lizard, a little tree frog and a shivering little hermit crab, all pressed against the sand.

6"Please," said the lizard. "The water is rising everywhere. Your burrow is the only deep, safe place on the whole lane."

7Big Claw clicked her claws. She thought about all the work she had done. She thought about how warm and dry it was inside. Then she looked at the hermit crab, whose shell was rocking in the wind.

8"Fine," she grumbled. "But no one touches my sea grapes."

9All night, the storm roared above them. Down in the burrow, it was quiet. The tree frog told stories. The lizard snored. The hermit crab fell asleep against Big Claw's leg, and she found she did not mind at all.

10In the morning, the sun came out and the visitors climbed into the bright, washed world.

11"Thank you," said the hermit crab. "Your burrow saved us."

12Big Claw watched them go. Then she began to dig a little wider, just in case.
