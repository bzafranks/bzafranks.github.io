Rebuilding the Reef

Original text by Barbara Zá Franks · 299 words

1The coral nursery looked nothing like Malik had imagined. He had pictured a garden. Instead, rows of white plastic frames, shaped like small trees, stood on the sandy bottom twenty feet below the boat, and from every branch hung a fragment of coral no bigger than his thumb.

2"Every one of those started as a broken piece," said Ms. Ferguson, the volunteer coordinator, as she checked his dive gear for the second time. "Storms snap them off. Boats break them. We collect the pieces that are still alive, hang them here where the water moves and the parrotfish can't nibble them, and wait."

3"How long?" Malik asked.

4"Months. Sometimes a year." She handed him a mesh bag. "Patience is most of the job."

5That morning, Malik's task was simple and, he soon discovered, maddening. He floated beside one frame with a soft brush and cleaned the algae that crept over the plastic, careful not to touch the living coral. The current kept nudging him off position. Twice he drifted into the next row and had to kick back, his breath loud in his ears. By the time Ms. Ferguson signaled to surface, he had cleaned only half a tree.

6On the boat, he pulled off his mask, discouraged. "That's it? A whole dive for half a frame?"

7Ms. Ferguson pointed to a clipboard covered in dates and numbers. "That frame went in last spring. Look." She showed him a photograph: a bare stub of coral the size of a fingernail. Then she pointed over the side, toward the tree he had been cleaning, where the same coral, now a branching fist of tan and gold, swayed in the current.

8Malik leaned over the rail for a long moment.

9"Next week," he said, "I want to try the whole frame."
