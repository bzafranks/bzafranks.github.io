Diggers of the Dry Season

Original text by Barbara Zá Franks · 264 words

1In parts of Africa, months can pass without rain. Rivers shrink and then disappear, leaving wide beds of dry sand. For the animals that live there, finding water is hard. But one animal knows a secret.

2African elephants can find water hidden under the sand. When a river dries up, some water often stays below the sand. Elephants dig for it with their feet and trunks. They scrape away sand until water seeps into the bottom of the hole. Then they drink, using their trunks like straws.

3When the elephants move on, the holes stay behind. Antelope, birds and smaller mammals visit them to drink. Many of them cannot dig deep enough to reach the water. In the driest months, an elephant's hole may be the only place nearby to drink.

4Elephants change their habitat in other ways, too. They push over trees to eat leaves and bark. This opens up space for grass to grow. Grazing animals then feed on the grass. Even an elephant's footprint can become a habitat. Scientists in Uganda found tiny water animals living in rainwater that had collected in elephant footprints.

5Not every change an elephant makes is welcome. When too many elephants live in one area, they can knock down so many trees that a woodland is damaged. Like other ecosystem engineers, elephants can help or harm. It depends on the place and how many elephants live there.

6Still, in a dry riverbed in the hottest part of the year, a hole dug by an elephant can save the lives of the animals that come after it.
