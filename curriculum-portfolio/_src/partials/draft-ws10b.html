Where Does Rain Come From?

Have you ever wondered where rain comes from? Rain is part of a never-ending journey called the water cycle. Water on Earth is always moving and changing from a liquid to a gas and back again. The water falling from the sky today may be the same water that once flowed in a river long ago.

The cycle begins with the sun. Sunlight warms water in oceans, lakes, and rivers. As the water heats up, some of it turns into an invisible gas called water vapor. This process is called evaporation. Plants also release water vapor through tiny openings in their leaves.

Next, water vapor rises into the sky, where the air is cooler. When the vapor cools, it changes back into tiny liquid droplets. These droplets gather together to form clouds. This change is called condensation. A cloud is really millions of these droplets floating in the air.

As more droplets join together, they become heavier. When they are too heavy to stay in the cloud, they fall to the ground. Falling water is called precipitation. Usually precipitation is rain. When the air is very cold, the water may fall as snow, sleet, or hail.

Where does the rain go? Some of it soaks into the ground and collects in underground spaces called aquifers. Some of it flows across the land into streams, rivers, and lakes. Eventually, much of it returns to the ocean. Then the sun warms the water again, and the cycle starts over.

Scientists call this process a cycle because it has no real beginning or end. Every raindrop you feel may once have been part of an ocean, a river, or even a plant. The next time it rains, think about the amazing journey those drops have taken!
