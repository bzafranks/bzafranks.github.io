I set the clean final as a student handout (passage, questions without the key, the cause-and-effect organizer and a cut-off exit ticket), printed it to US Letter PDF, and checked it in color and in grayscale with background printing turned off, since that is how most school copiers run. The first proof had six problems. All six are fixed in the version you can download.

Proof 1 findingWhy it mattersFix in proof 2
Answer lines were drawn as a background pattern. With backgrounds off, every writing line disappeared, and even in color two questions printed with no lines.Students get blank space with no guide for how much to write.Lines rebuilt as ruled borders, which print with backgrounds off.
The "Exit ticket" heading sat alone at the bottom of page 2, with its questions on page 3.A stranded heading, and the ticket could not be cut off and collected.Exit ticket moved to its own boxed section with a name line, and headings set to stay with what follows.
Paragraph numbers printed as small grey superscripts, about 8.6 pt.Three questions send students to a paragraph by number. They need to find it quickly.Numbers set inline, bold and darker, here and on every passage in the portfolio.
The title appeared twice: once as the page heading and again inside the passage box.Looks like two texts.The repeated title is hidden on the handout.
Standard codes such as (RI.4.1) printed after each student question.Codes are for the teacher and add clutter for nine-year-olds.Codes removed from the student copy. They stay in the teacher's answer key above.
Labels inside the organizer boxes printed at about 8 pt.Hard to read, and the boxes give no hint where to start.Labels raised to about 10.5 pt, and the directions for the written explanation now sit under the organizer.

Proof 2: four pages. The passage is on page 1, the questions on page 2, the organizer and written response on page 3, and the exit ticket on page 4 as a half sheet. Body text is 12 pt, every line prints in grayscale, and no heading is separated from its content. One thing I left as it is: page 3 is about half empty, because I kept the organizer and its writing lines together rather than splitting them. Fonts fall back to whatever the printing computer has, so a district template would name and embed its own typefaces.
