The Crunching Sound

Kiara floated face down in the clear water off Taino Beach, breathing through her snorkel. Below her, the reef spread out like a garden: purple sea fans, brain coral as round as a pumpkin, and fish everywhere.

Then she heard it. Crunch. Crunch. Crunch.

She lifted her head. "Uncle Desmond! Something down there is breaking the coral!"

Her uncle swam over and pushed his mask up. He had been diving on this reef since he was younger than Kiara. "Show me."

Kiara pointed. A fish as long as her arm, bright blue with flashes of pink, was pressed against a rock. Each time it opened its mouth, there was that sound again. Crunch.

"It's wrecking the reef," Kiara said. "Can't we stop it?"

Uncle Desmond smiled around his snorkel. "Watch a little longer."

The fish moved along the rock, biting and scraping. Then a small white cloud drifted out behind it and sank slowly to the bottom.

Kiara wrinkled her nose. "Did it just..."

"It did," her uncle laughed. "That's a parrotfish. It scrapes off the green stuff, the algae, and it swallows bits of rock too. Then it grinds the rock up and, well, you saw. That white cloud is sand."

Kiara stared at the white sand on the bottom. She thought of the beach behind her, long and pale and soft.

"So the beach..." she began.

"Some of it, yes. And by eating the algae, the parrotfish keeps it from smothering the coral. That crunching is one of the sounds of a healthy reef."

Kiara put her face back in the water. The parrotfish was still working, crunch after crunch. This time she did not want to stop it. She wanted to say thank you.
